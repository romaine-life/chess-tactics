# ADR-0358: A premove is judged against permanent board law, not the current position

## Context and Problem Statement

A premove was generated by `legalMoves` on the live board, widened by one special case: a square
held by a friendly piece could be targeted as a speculative recapture. Everything else had to be
legal **right now**.

That is the wrong question. A premove is queued while the opponent is thinking, and it answers the
position their move is about to create. Judging it against the position on screen refuses exactly
the predictions worth making:

- the rook cannot be sent down its file, because its own pawn is standing in the way — the pawn
  that is about to be traded off;
- the pawn cannot be pointed at the square it wants to capture on, because nothing is standing
  there yet — the whole point being that something is about to;
- the king cannot step onto a square that is attacked today by a piece that is about to be
  captured, pinned, or blocked;
- en passant cannot be pre-committed, because the double step hasn't happened.

The player could see the position they were playing against and could not express it.

## Decision Drivers

- A premove is a *prediction*. The only positions worth premoving against are ones that do not
  exist yet.
- Nothing is risked by a generous queue: firing is a separate step with its own exact test.
- Some of the board never changes. Terrain, elevation, water, edge fences and rock obstacles are
  the same on every ply of the match, so a square unreachable through them is unreachable forever
  — offering it could only ever queue a move that cannot fire.
- Chess.com and lichess set the player's expectation: premove geometry there ignores occupancy
  outright, including sliding straight through pieces.

## Decision Outcome

Chosen: **a premove is offered for every square the unit's own geometry reaches through the
board's permanent layers, and nothing about the current position constrains it.**

`rules.speculativeMoves` is the queue-time generator, beside `legalMoves` rather than derived from
it. It applies:

- board bounds and the piece's movement geometry;
- terrain, elevation, and water (a slide may end on water, never pass it);
- edge fences;
- obstacles — rocks and neutral pieces are board furniture that never moves, dies, or is captured,
  so they block a ray exactly like terrain.

and deliberately ignores:

- **blockers** — a ray slides through every movable piece, friendly or hostile;
- **the destination's occupant** — it may hold anything, or nothing;
- **pawn capture geometry** — both diagonals are always offered, which covers both a capture that
  hasn't happened yet and en passant;
- **king safety** — a premove may walk into a check that the opponent's own move is about to
  dissolve;
- **castling's dynamic conditions** — the squares between being clear, the destinations being
  clear, and the check/crossed-square tests. The authored rule, both pieces unmoved on their
  squares, and terrain/fence travel still bind.

Legality returns, unchanged and exact, when the step fires: the store's premove drain re-validates
the head against the real board through `legalMoves`, and a chain whose prediction reality did not
honour is dropped whole, as before. The projection layer follows the same rule — folding a
speculative step removes whoever stands on the destination, because "by the time I arrive that
square is mine" is what the plan asserts.

## Consequences

- Good: the player can queue the position they are predicting — slide through a blocker, aim a
  pawn at an empty square, pre-commit en passant, castle out of a formation that is about to
  clear.
- Good: one rule replaces a special case. Speculative recapture is no longer a carve-out; it falls
  out of ignoring occupancy, and it can no longer be the *only* prediction the game accepts.
- Good: nothing illegal ever executes. The honest test moved to the only place it was ever load-
  bearing, and the two now say different things on purpose.
- Cost: more squares light up during the opponent's turn, and a premove is easier to queue and
  lose. That is the trade every chess client makes.
- Cost: with a unit selected, clicking one of your own pieces that lies on its speculative path
  queues a premove onto it rather than switching selection (pre-existing precedence, now reached
  far more often). Clicking a premoved unit's origin square still selects it, and Escape still
  clears the chain.

## Verification

- `premoves.test.ts` pins both halves: sliding onto AND past a friendly blocker, a pawn's diagonal
  onto an empty square, a king onto a currently-attacked square — against a rock that still stops
  the ray it stands on, and geometry that still refuses a square off the rook's lines.
- The existing drain tests are unchanged and still pass, which is the point: fire-time legality
  did not move.
- Live capture from `/play` with a rook, a friendly pawn on its file, and the opponent to move:
  the highlighted target set is the full file including and beyond the pawn, while the rank stops
  at the edge of the level's `void` terrain.
